# Words we use

Tailr is for job seekers, not for us. Every screen, email and notification uses the plain
words below. Code may keep its internal names (`brief`, `radar`, `kit`, `fit`); people never
see them.

## The idea in one breath

Add your CV. Tell Tailr what job you want. Every morning it searches LinkedIn and JobStreet
and shows every job on your **Jobs** page, best match first. For a job you like, it prepares
your application (a CV and cover letter written from your real experience). You apply, and
track it under **My applications**.

## Style

- Say what happens, in the user's terms: "Tailr searches LinkedIn and JobStreet every
  morning at 7:00", not "the brief is dispatched".
- One idea per sentence. Sentence case. No middle dots, no ALL CAPS labels.
- Every empty screen says what to do next, with the button to do it.
- Explain a number where it appears: "86% match: based on your skills, job title,
  experience, location and pay."
- Avoid the tailoring metaphors in instructions ("measure", "stitch", "fit", "tape"); the
  brand can keep them in the logo and the loading animation.
- Slow work says what's happening and that you can carry on: "Drafting… About 20 seconds.
  You can keep working; your draft will show up here."
- Where only the person can add a detail, ask in square brackets rather than guess:
  "[how you divided the work]".
- Text written by the AI is labelled ("Tailr's take", "Tailr's suggestion", "Tailr wrote
  this from your profile"), never just styled.
